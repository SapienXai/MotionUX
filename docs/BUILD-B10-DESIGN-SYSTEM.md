# Studio and project identity

The Studio shell retains neutral tool surfaces, a violet selection accent, and its own persisted System, Light and Dark appearance. A project's appearance applies within an authored application boundary. The same boundary renders in the live canvas and standalone Next.js.

| Preset | Color direction | Heading family | Spacing | Corners |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Editorial Calm | Paper, ink and olive-brown actions | Georgia serif | Generous, 24 px rhythm | Crisp, 4 px |
| Product Precision | Cool blue surfaces and deep blue actions | System sans | Compact, 12 px rhythm | Rounded, 12 px |
| Warm Operations | Clay and rose-neutral surfaces | Humanist sans | Balanced, 16 px rhythm | Soft, 18 px |

The strict v1 identity references a preset and typed density, heading family and corner choices. The owned CSS maps semantic ground, surface, raised, border, ink, secondary text, action and focus tokens into the app's components. Applications retain their domain content and component structure. Without an identity the application follows the original template styling. Theme preference selects the corresponding palette. A preview does not write to project history, and Apply issues one undoable document edit.

Keep UI chrome and application color roles scoped. Light and dark, selected and hover text, high-contrast focus, mobile widths and no-motion states require real screenshots and computed checks. Do not pass styling regressions by loosening accessibility or layout assertions.
